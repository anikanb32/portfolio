I'm interested in the moment where a system becomes too complex for a person to understand — and the design decisions that determine whether they trust it or abandon it.

I'm Anika — a product designer specializing in complex, intelligent systems. I design the interfaces where people need to understand, control, and trust things that are inherently difficult to see: AI agents acting autonomously, spatial computing layering information onto the physical world, assistive technology supporting cognition.

My path to this work started with neuroscience. At Carnegie Mellon, I studied how the brain processes and organizes information — how we build mental models, how attention works, what happens when cognitive load exceeds capacity. That foundation shapes everything I design: I think about interfaces as cognitive environments, not just visual ones.

Now at UC Berkeley's School of Information, I'm focused on intelligent systems design — how AI and human decision-making intersect, and what the interaction paradigms for that should even look like. My research spans context-aware AR/VR interfaces, human-AI interaction patterns, and accessibility in AI systems.

In industry, I've designed AI agent orchestration interfaces at IBM, worked on context-aware augmented reality at Meta Reality Labs, explored immersive automotive interactions at BMW, and shipped products for assistive technology and custom manufacturing.

Outside of systems design, I make things with my hands — fashion collections, field observations, community projects. I think the best interface designers understand form and material, not just pixels. Garment construction taught me that structure is always communicating something, whether you intend it to or not. The same is true of an AI agent's interface.
