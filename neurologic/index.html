Overview

A tool for users to understand neuroscience and the brain in a more interactive way.

NeuroLogic is an interactive website designed to help users gain a foundational understanding of neuroscience and brain function.

It is important for us to pose essential questions about the brain's nature, our understanding of it, and the link between human behavior and brain patterns.

Problem

The complexity of neuroscience creates barriers for learning.

Neuroscience is inherently complex, with intricate concepts about brain function, neural pathways, and cognitive processes that can be overwhelming for beginners.

The problem sits in the disconnect between the exciting nature of brain science and the intimidating presentation of it.



Early Wireframes

Creating a general layout of information for each page to establish the foundation for interactive learning.

I added a home page for basic navigation and posing broad questions. The goal of the brain areas page was to create an interactive tool to view the brain and localizations of different units. The simulation page will show how various stimuli can change brain activity, giving users the ability to toggle various factors. I also added resources to further the educational motivation.

Prototyping

Testing out the Concept

I conducted usability tests to address two main parts: functionality and design evaluation. Participants were guided through key tasks using a think-aloud protocol to observe their navigation and interaction with the website.

Feedback + Design Changes

There was valuable feedback for enhancing user interaction, simplifying complex content, and improving accessibility. A key suggestion was to streamline interactions by developing the brain interface with D3.js, making sure that visuals and controls are intuitive and well-explained while minimizing unnecessary features.

I received recommendations to use both color and size variations for highlighting important information, making the platform more inclusive for users with color vision deficiencies. The feedback emphasized using images of the brain to illustrate different slices and activity areas, which would help users understand the complex structure more intuitively.

Refining the Design

Based on the feedback received, I created a second iteration of wireframes that incorporated the suggested improvements for better user interaction and accessibility.

Implementation

Design Evolution and Technical Challenges

I shifted from an initial "brighter white and blue theme" to a "black, white, grayscales, and pops of red" to create a more dramatic effect and clearer contrast, ensuring text and buttons are distinguishable without relying solely on color variations.

One of the biggest challenges was integrating the external brain visual using three.js, managing the terminal, eliminating errors, and cleaning up outdated code. This required learning different rendering processes and leveraging generative AI to create accurate visuals, overcoming the complexities of rendering them manually.

Interaction

Three core interactive features that bring neuroscience concepts to life

Brain Areas

Allows users to explore different regions of the brain and understand their functions. The 3D interactive model lets users rotate and zoom in/out to view from different angles. Red dots on specific brain areas can be clicked to display their functions in an adjacent text box.

Simulation

How various factors like stimulants and sleep affect neuron activity. Separate simulation tabs for Sugar, Caffeine, and Sleep allow users to focus on specific factors like dopamine sensitivity, stimulant levels, environmental effects, and sensitivity history.

Resources

Provides additional materials and references for users interested in expanding their neuroscience knowledge with flippable cards, links, and journal papers.

Final Thoughts

Learnings...

Creating NeuroLogic taught me the power of storytelling in educational technology. Though the challenge of crafting a narrative that makes complex neuroscience concepts accessible and interesting, I learned how visual design and interaction patterns can influence navigation and retention.

With more time...

I would expand the simulation capabilities to include more brain regions, and focus more on neurological conditions and their visual representations. I'd conduct more comprehensive user research with different demographics to understand how diverse learning styles interact with the platform.
