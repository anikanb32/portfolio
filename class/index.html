Overview

A Personalized Dashboard for Program Management

For my HCI capstone project, we partnered with Community Living and Support Services, also known as CLASS, a nonprofit in Pittsburgh, PA.

We designed a solution that reduces staff burden by consolidating participant information, empowering participants to engage with their goals and activities and reduce staff admin work.

Solution

Participant Survey

Participants choose activities of interest

Through morning check-ins, participants can choose activities or add their own.
Activity Suggestions

Staff can plan activities efficiently

Based on today’s participants, staff can plan activities of interest and match skill levels.
Participant Profile

Quickly learn details about participants

Staff can reference participant profiles to learn more about history, skills, and goals at a glance.

Impact

Currently empowering 15+ individuals with disabilities at CLASS to personalize their growth.

LIVE
The tool is live and is currently being used by the Structured Day Program staff and participants.

Context

Communication is critical for individuals with disabilities or cognitive impairments.

CLASS is a nonprofit organization that helps individuals with disabilities live independently in their communities and achieve their goals. Community plays a vital role in the rehabilitation and growth process for individuals with cognitive impairments and motor disabilities, which is exactly what CLASS facilitates.

The goal of our project was to research and develop a product that would help any program at CLASS. This was a very broad scope with seemingly unlimited directions to take. So, our first step was to dive into discovery.

Discovery

How can we support increased independence for individuals with Traumatic Brain Injuries?

We wanted to understand how the program operates and surface the daily challenges faced by CLASS staff and participants.

Research helped us uncover the fragmented nature of the program.

We identified three main insights about how the program currently functions. So our next step was to define our problem space given these particular gaps.


Problem



Ideation

What if all this scattered program information lived in one place?

I ideated multiple prototyping directions for a tool to consolidate all this paperwork and activity information that staff manage.

Prototyping

Initial prototypes highlighted 3 main features for the staff workflow.

The most important requirements for staff are the ability to view attendance, quickly glance at participant profiles to see skills and goals, and to find activity suggestions for the day.

Usability Testing

But... the dashboard was missing real-time participant input.

Usability testing uncovered that staff Staff still need to go back and forth between attendance, profiles, and activities to effectively plan for the day.

Iterations

Amplifying the 'voice' of participants.

Final Designs

Meet the dashboard that makes communication seamless.

All the participant info staff need is now in one place. No scattered files, playing phone tag with coworkers, and spending excess time planning useful activities.

Outcomes

Less fragmentation and more time with people.

We met the goal of centralizing information, making it easier for staff to plan and view critical information. Participants loved being able to advocate for their interests and staff were better equipped to make decisions for the program flow.

Final Thoughts

Learning...

I realized how important it is to give people autonomy through design, especially when their voices are not always heard.


Impactful design work can sometimes happen behind the scenes, empowering the people who directly support those who need it most.

With more time...

I would want to run user testing with the most refined coded product specifically to test metrics on staff time spent planning and researching daily activities.


Expand the capabilities of the participant-facing tool to adapt to the diverse needs of individuals.
